Korea's biggest holiday falls on Friday, 25 September 2026, with the official break running 24-27 September. Tickets for the trains that carry the country home go on sale to the general public from 7 to 11 September, each day between 07:00 and 13:00 KST, and only through the Korail+ app or the holiday booking site.

Two things changed this year. Korail and SR merged on 1 September, so every high-speed train is now branded KTX and SRT departures from Suseo are booked in Korail+, not the old SRT app. And fares fell about 10 percent: Seoul to Busan dropped from 59,800 to 54,400 won.

The sale is split across five mornings

Not everything opens on day one. Conventional trains come first, then KTX routes are released by departure station. Showing up on the wrong morning means missing your route entirely.

The tickets on sale cover trains running 23-27 September, which includes the day before the holiday begins, when outbound traffic peaks.

Booking is online only

There is no station queue for holiday tickets. Reservations run through the Korail+ mobile app and a dedicated holiday web page. Riders who qualify as mobility-disadvantaged had an earlier window on 3-4 September and could also book by phone through the customer centre on 1588-7788.

What the merger means for your fare

The 1 September integration aligned high-speed fares to the lower SRT level, so the cut is real money on the routes foreign visitors use most.

Capacity rose as well: roughly 15,000 extra high-speed seats on weekdays and up to 17,000 at weekends, with Suseo station gaining about 30 percent. Departures from Suseo also earn 5 percent of the payment back as mileage.

Paying for a seat you already reserved

Reserving is not buying. Payment opens at 00:00 on 12 September, and general-sale reservations must be paid by 15 September or they are cancelled automatically and passed to the waiting list. Tickets from the earlier accessible-booking window have until 18 September.

That gap is easy to forget: a seat secured on 7 September cannot be paid for until the 12th, then expires three days later.

If you miss the window

Cancelled seats return to the pool throughout the period, and the waiting-list function in Korail+ assigns them in order. Express buses and domestic flights absorb the overflow, though both fill quickly for 24 and 25 September.

Planning around the four days

Chuseok itself is 25 September. The holiday runs Thursday to Sunday, and there is no substitute holiday attached this year, so normal business resumes on Monday 28 September. Expect intercity roads and stations to be heaviest on the 24th outbound and the 27th inbound.

Questions travellers ask

Can I book Seoul-Busan KTX on 7 September?

No. The 7th covers conventional trains only. Seoul-Busan KTX is released last, on 11 September.

Does the SRT app still work?

Not for these tickets. Since the 1 September merger, Suseo departures are booked in Korail+ along with everything else.

What happens if I do not pay by 15 September?

The reservation is cancelled automatically and offered to waiting-list holders. Payment cannot be made before 12 September, so the usable window is four days.

Is 28 September a holiday too?

No. The break is 24-27 September only, and no substitute holiday follows.
